Vector control hardware · in development

A device that finds mosquitoes and takes them out of the air.

Built for the places where mosquito-borne disease actually kills people — not for suburban patios. Anophelyze is an early-stage hardware company developing a patent-pending system for vector mitigation in malaria- and dengue-endemic regions.

Engagement sequence Simulation · not to scale

The system

Three problems, one machine.

Each is hard on its own. Solving them together — in something that has to run unattended, survive heat and dust, tolerate unreliable power, and cost little enough to deploy at scale — is the work.

Detection

Picking a mosquito out of everything else moving through a field at dusk, when the mosquitoes are most active and the light is worst.

Engagement

Acting on a target fast enough that it can't escape, without the air-current warning that lets a mosquito ride out a swat.

Safety

Eye-safe by design, around people and livestock. No fire risk, no corneal or retinal damage, no burns — the constraint every other decision is built around.

Why this matters

The deadliest animal on earth is an insect.

Malaria, dengue, yellow fever, and Zika cause preventable death and life-disrupting illness across sub-Saharan Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and Latin America, and hit young children hardest. Insecticide-treated nets and indoor spraying have saved millions of lives, but their gains have stalled against insecticide resistance and vectors that bite outdoors and early in the evening. Anophelyze was founded in 2026, after several years of preparation, out of a need for environmentally neutral approaches that work where those tools don't.
